**Bottom line.** Ireland's Land Development Agency — LDA, the state's main public housing body — delivered about 850 homes in 2023, its only fully audited year. That is around 3 percent of national completions. Every single one was purchased from private developers, not built by the Agency. Even if it hits its own 2028 target, it will still be delivering about 3 percent of the national annual goal.

## The Question

The Land Development Agency was established by statute in September 2018 as the Irish state's flagship vehicle for delivering affordable and cost-rental housing on public and acquired land. It began substantive delivery in 2023, once its forward-purchase programme — branded Project Tosaigh — came online. Given the scale of Ireland's housing shortfall, the obvious question is: how many homes has the Agency actually delivered, and how big a dent does that make in the Housing for All target of 50,500 homes a year?

## What we found

Only one year — 2023 — has an audited delivery figure, and it is about 850 homes. That works out to 2.6 to 3.5 percent of all national completions, depending on which official completions series is used. Cumulative delivery through the end of 2024 is about 2,054 homes.

- Every one of the 850 homes delivered in 2023 was acquired through forward-purchase — bought from private developers who had already built or were nearly finished building them. The Agency built zero homes directly in 2023.
- The Agency's first direct-build project, at Shanganagh in County Dublin, was not due to complete its first units until 2025.
- Implied 2024 delivery was about 1,200 homes. Press estimates for 2025 fall between 1,200 and 1,800. Cumulative delivery through end-2025 is therefore in the 3,200 to 3,900 range.
- The Agency's own published target for 2028 is 8,000 homes cumulatively — not the "14,000 by 2028" figure commonly cited in commentary.
- Even if the 8,000 target is met exactly, it averages about 1,600 homes a year — around 3 percent of the national Housing for All target.

## Why that matters

Because the Agency operated entirely through forward-purchase in its only audited year, any headline claim about its "contribution to national delivery" has a structural double-counting problem. The 850 homes were built by private developers under the normal planning regime. They would have been built regardless. The Agency's contribution is about *tenure* — converting market-rate units into cost-rental and affordable-purchase units — not about *additionality*, meaning homes that would not otherwise exist.

That distinction is rarely drawn in public debate. The 2.6 to 3.5 percent share is an attribution share. The additionality share, for 2023 at least, is close to zero.

The paper also tidies up a set of widely repeated numbers. The precise "854" sometimes cited for 2023 is a false precision; the Agency itself says "ca. 850". Year-by-year figures for 2018 to 2022 that circulate in secondary sources were reverse-engineered from a single cumulative total and do not correspond to anything in the published annual reports. The "14,000 by 2028" target is not in the 2023 annual report — the actual figure is 8,000.

## What it means in practice

**For anyone writing about Irish housing policy.** The citable numbers are: about 850 homes delivered in 2023 (audited), about 2,054 cumulative through end-2024 (press source), and 8,000 cumulative by 2028 (the Agency's own target). Tenure matters too: the Agency's cost-rental and affordable-purchase output may be more significant than the raw share suggests, because it reaches households that market-rate supply does not.

**For policymakers.** The Agency is running at roughly 850 to 1,500 homes a year. Its own 2028 target is internally consistent with that pace provided direct-build comes online on schedule. But even at the target, the Agency represents a small share of the national framework. This is not a failure — it was never designed to be the lead delivery vehicle. It is, however, inconsistent with the common framing that the Agency "will solve the housing crisis". The arithmetic does not support that claim.

**For voters.** The Agency is contributing real homes, in the low thousands per year, in tenures that would otherwise be underserved. It is not, and was not set up to be, the main engine of Irish housing delivery.

## How we did it

We used the [Land Development Agency 2023 Annual Report](https://lda.ie/) as the only audited delivery source, cross-referenced against the Irish Times cumulative figure for end-2024 and press reporting for 2025. For the denominator we used the [Central Statistics Office](https://data.cso.ie/) new-dwelling-completions series in both its towns-only and all-Ireland versions, because they give materially different share figures. The analysis is descriptive comparison with explicit uncertainty flags on unaudited rows — not a predictive modelling exercise. Every share figure is labelled as attribution, not additionality, to avoid the double-counting inherent in forward-purchase delivery.
