## The Question

The Land Development Agency was established by statute in September 2018 as the Irish state's flagship vehicle for delivering affordable and cost-rental housing on public and acquired land. It began substantive delivery in 2023, once its forward-purchase programme (Project Tosaigh) came online. Given the scale of Ireland's housing shortfall, a simple but important question is: how many homes has the Agency actually delivered, and what share of the 50,500-per-year Housing for All target does that represent?

## What We Found

**Only one year — 2023 — has an audited Agency delivery figure, and it is about 850 homes.** That represents 2.6-3.5% of all national completions, depending on which completions series is used as the denominator. Cumulative delivery through end-2024 was approximately 2,054 homes.

- Every one of the 850 homes delivered in 2023 was acquired via the Agency's forward-purchase programme, which buys completed or near-complete homes from private developers. The Agency built zero homes directly in 2023.
- The Agency's first direct-build project (Shanganagh in County Dublin) was not expected to complete its first homes until 2025.
- Implied 2024 delivery was roughly 1,200 homes; 2025 delivery is press-estimated in the range of 1,200 to 1,800. Cumulative end-2025 delivery is therefore in the 3,200 to 3,900 range.
- The Agency's own forward target from the 2023 annual report is 8,000 homes by 2028 — not the "14,000 by 2028" figure commonly cited in media commentary.
- Even if the 8,000-by-2028 target is met exactly, it represents an average of roughly 1,600 homes per year — about 3% of the national Housing for All target of 50,500 per year.

## Why That's Surprising

Because the Agency operates entirely through forward-purchase in its only audited year, there is a structural double-counting problem in any headline claim about "Agency contribution to national housing delivery." The homes the Agency acquired were built by private developers under the normal planning regime — they would have been built regardless of whether the Agency existed. The Agency's contribution is about *tenure* (converting market-rate units into cost-rental and affordable-purchase units) rather than about *additionality* (adding homes that would not otherwise exist).

That distinction is rarely made in public discussion. The 2.6-3.5% share of national completions is an attribution share — the fraction of completions the Agency acquired — not an additionality share. The additionality share is, by this logic, closer to zero, at least for 2023.

The paper also corrects a set of widely-repeated figures. The precise "854" sometimes cited for 2023 is a false precision; the Agency itself says "ca. 850." Year-by-year figures for 2018-2022 that have circulated in secondary sources were reverse-engineered back from a single cumulative total and do not correspond to any published annual report. The "14,000 by 2028" target in circulation is not in the 2023 annual report — the real target is 8,000.

## What It Means

For policy realism, the Agency is running at a roughly 850-to-1,500-homes-per-year delivery pace. Its own 2028 target is internally consistent with that trajectory provided the direct-build pipeline comes online on schedule. But even at the target, the Agency represents a small share of the national housing framework. This is not a failure: the Agency was never designed to be the lead delivery vehicle. It is, however, inconsistent with the common framing that "the Land Development Agency will solve the housing crisis." The arithmetic does not support that claim.

For anyone writing about Irish housing policy, the numbers to cite are: about 850 homes delivered in 2023 (audited), about 2,054 cumulative through end-2024 (press source), and 8,000 cumulative by 2028 (the Agency's own target). Tenure matters too: the Agency's cost-rental and affordable-purchase output may be more significant than the raw share suggests, because it reaches households that market-rate supply does not.

For a voter or commuter, the headline is simple: the Agency is contributing real homes, in the low thousands per year, in tenures that would otherwise be underserved. It is not, and was not set up to be, the main engine of Irish housing delivery.

## How We Did It

We used the [Land Development Agency 2023 Annual Report](https://lda.ie/) as the only audited delivery source, cross-referenced against the Irish Times cumulative figure for end-2024 and press reporting for 2025. For the denominator we used the [Central Statistics Office](https://data.cso.ie/) new-dwelling-completions series (both the towns-only and all-Ireland versions, because they give materially different share figures). The analysis is descriptive comparison with explicit uncertainty flags on unaudited rows — not a modelling exercise. Share figures are clearly labelled as attribution, not additionality, to avoid the double-counting inherent in forward-purchase delivery.
